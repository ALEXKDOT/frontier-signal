## Research and select

### Independent AI Watch mandate

The original repeated task watched for significant developments in frontier AI safety and loss-of-control risk, and asked what would materially change a five-year threat assessment. Carry that reasoning forward independently. The original chat is historical provenance only and its recurring task is not a dependency.

- Search for autonomous agent incidents; sandbox and containment escapes; unauthorized persistence, replication, and resource acquisition; deceptive or monitor-evasive behavior; frontier cyber capability; AI-assisted AI R&D and recursive improvement; model-weight theft or proliferation; major CBRN capability changes; and major U.S. and international governance changes. Search for effective safeguards and risk reductions with equal care.
- For each material development, explain what happened, how credible the evidence is, which pathway changes, and whether the five-year outlook becomes more concerning, less concerning, mixed, or unchanged. Distinguish serious operational/cyber emergencies from irreversible loss of human control; evidence for one does not automatically establish the other.
- Separate reported observations, interpretation, and forecasts. State which prior assumption strengthened or weakened and why. Distinguish accidental access from intentional circumvention; authorized tests from deployment behavior; temporary external access from durable persistence; failed automatic stopping from survival of a determined human shutdown; supervised AI-assisted research from autonomous recursive improvement.
- Assess competitive incentives, disclosure delays, independent verification, and implementation. Give more weight to demonstrated controls and binding, externally verifiable commitments than aspirations. Do not infer prevalence from raw disclosure counts without a denominator or confuse repeated coverage with independent corroboration.
- Weigh counterevidence in the same update: successful human intervention, fixes, independent evaluations, enforced pauses, and implemented governance. Explain the limits of those safeguards as well as their benefits. Do not force a directional change to make an update sound important.
- In the existing interpretation fields, provide a concise conclusion, the strongest reason for concern, the strongest counterpoint, what remains unproven, and observable evidence that would raise or lower concern next. The weekly history summary should explain the net qualitative change and its drivers, including an unchanged assessment when justified. Keep language accessible and non-sensational.
- Maintain a rolling five-year horizon from the review date and identify the horizon when making an outlook statement. Do not inherit the chat's subjective percentages or treat its old end-of-2031 horizon as permanently current. Keep the app's outlook unrated and publish clearly labeled qualitative interpretation, not an invented numerical risk score or human approval.

### Broader source review

1. Read `dist/data/signals.json` and any incomplete release state. Start from `updates.lastCheckedAt` or `asOf`, with a seven-day overlap for delayed reporting. If a run was missed, catch up from that cursor rather than assuming the last seven days is sufficient.
2. Search the web and read original sources. Prioritize developer incident reports and system cards from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta, and other relevant frontier developers; METR, UK AI Security Institute, US CAISI/NIST, Transluce, Apollo Research, and other credible independent investigations; and official government/regulatory publications. Use reputable reporting for discovery and corroboration. Do not treat search snippets, syndicated copies, model output, social speculation, or the old chat as verification of a new claim.
3. Select ordinarily 3–8 material developments, fewer when warranted. Do not fill a quota. Prioritize real impact, new mechanisms or capabilities, strength of evidence, scale, and whether a development changes a risk pathway. Include meaningful de-risking: effective controls, fixes, operational pauses, independent verification, and implemented governance. Routine model releases belong in the neutral model catalog below; add a risk record only when they have material safety relevance.
4. Cover autonomy, containment, cyber, monitorability/deception, AI-assisted AI R&D, model proliferation, CBRN, and governance. Distinguish human-directed misuse, authorized security research, evaluation incidents, and autonomous unauthorized actions. Keep biological/cyber descriptions non-operational.
5. Open and check every source supporting a new record. At least one accessible primary report or credible original investigation is required. If the evidence is insufficient, keep the candidate in `editorial/drafts/`; do not publish it merely to fill the weekly update. Identify attribution uncertainty and publisher conflicts. Never manufacture a source, date, reviewer, incident, or probability.
